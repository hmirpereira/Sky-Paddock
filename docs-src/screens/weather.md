# Weather screens

- **Hazards**: yellow, orange and red warnings from IPMA (the Portuguese weather service) for the district chosen in the settings, kept in Portuguese on purpose and without accents (the Star4000 fonts have none). Skipped when there are none or no district is set. In widescreen the warning colour fills the whole width. The bottom line shows the three most severe warnings too, one line each after the weather lines: a label in the colour of the level (`AVISO LARANJA`, white letters) and the type with the end time (`Precipitacao, ate TER 10 NOV 16:45`, or `de ... ate ...` before it starts). When the text does not fit it crawls to the left like a breaking news headline, but only the text: the label stays in view (`utils/hazard-lines.mjs`: the Hazards module hands over its warnings after each download, which needs the district set in the settings).
- **Local Radar**: the rain from [RainViewer](https://www.rainviewer.com) (the last 13 images, 10 minutes apart, played three times; zoom 7 on the location, the most the free service gives) over an Esri map (`World_Ocean_Base`) centred on the location, at 60% so the map shows through; the city names (Esri `World_Light_Gray_Reference`, same host) and, drawn by the page itself from `utils/radar-lines.mjs`, the coast of Iberia (white with a dark edge), the whole land border of Portugal (solid) and the lines between its 18 districts (dashed) are drawn over the rain, so the map can be read even under widespread rain (the lines come from Natural Earth 1:10m, public domain, simplified to about 300 m by a generator script outside the repository; the Azores, Madeira and the Spanish provinces have none); the location with its temperature and today's icon on top. The map fills the screen between the header and the bottom line, as the other screens: the header, the source line (small, bottom right) and the countdown line are over it. The colour scale is in the top right corner, with the time of the frame under it (no box, no title bar). The latest image is held for 2 seconds each time it comes round and labelled `NOW`; the earlier ones `PAST`. No forecast frames are shown. The rain uses the Universal Blue colour scheme (the legend: drizzle in beige, light to moderate rain in cyan and blue, heavy in yellow, orange and red). No lightning is drawn: the GOES lightning data does not reach Portugal. The map tiles are loaded once per start.
- **Travel Forecast**: two pages of 10 seconds, no scrolling. Page 1: the cities in `TRAVEL_CITIES` (`utils/travel-cities.mjs`, today Frankfurt, Munich, Zurich, Geneva and Vienna) in one Open-Meteo request; after 18:00 it shows tomorrow. Page 2, laid out as Current Conditions and inside the same box: temperature, condition and icon, wind and gusts, then the city with the 8-bit flag of its country and its local time, feels like, humidity, dewpoint, visibility, pressure (with the rising or falling arrow) and cloud cover now (your units), of the city chosen with **Travel Forecast city** in the settings (a list grouped by continent, "-- Europe --" and "-- North America --", each in alphabetical order, as "London - United Kingdom"), from 30 European and Canadian cities in `DETAIL_CITIES` (same file, none of them on page 1; Montreal by default). If one page has no data the other is shown alone.
- **Regional Forecast**: the original used the US National Weather Service and a US map, so it never worked outside the USA. Here it shows the 18 Portuguese district capitals and the island capitals (Ponta Delgada, Angra, Horta, Funchal) on six maps (North, Centre, Lisbon, South, Madeira, Azores), 6 seconds each, under the title National Forecast with the zone on the second line. Each city has a small white dot on its exact place, and its label sits beside it; when two would cover each other, one moves to a free place next to its city. Today's forecast until 18:00, then tomorrow's, in one Open-Meteo request. The maps are Esri's ocean tiles without names, with the coast and border lines of the Local Radar, and its district lines solid and thinner than the border (`utils/regional-map.mjs`). Madeira and the Azores have a map each, with their coast (`utils/island-lines.mjs`, Natural Earth 1:10m, public domain). The city coordinates are in `utils/regional-pt.mjs`, made by a generator script (not in this repository); its map images are no longer used.
- **Air & Pollen**: on the left, a dial with the European Air Quality Index for the current hour (0 to 150, the bands in the colours of the European Environment Agency, the number and the band in the middle); on the right, the daily pollen averages from Open-Meteo / CAMS (Europe only) as a level and four steps, with the US National Allergy Bureau scales as a guide. One request for both; until 1.6 the air quality had a screen of its own (US index). Outside Europe only the air quality is shown.
