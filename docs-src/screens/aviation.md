# Aviation screens

Navy and silver, with yellow flight numbers. Apart from the METAR, they read their data from a small service on the Raspberry Pi (see [The Raspberry Pi side](../../README.md#the-raspberry-pi-side)).

**Work mode** (a setting, for a break room): only Work Schedule (its Gates page, of everybody's gates), Departures, Cancellations, Overhead (its radar page and the airport plan, centred on the airport of the settings) and Airport METAR; see [Look and feel](../look-and-feel.md).

**Before a shift**: when the next shift of the Work Schedule starts in 3 hours or less, the aviation screens (Work Schedule, Departures, Cancellations, Overhead and, last, Airport METAR, those that are on and have data) go round 4 times, one after the other, before the rotation moves on to the next screen. A shift that has already begun does not count. With no schedule (no Pi service) or with the next shift further away, the rotation is as usual.

- **Departures**: the departures of the day from the airport set on the Pi, for the airlines set there, in the style of the Gates page (its font, the lighter band on every other flight, the tail icon (75% of the height of its row, with a thin white outline of 1 px, also on the Gates and Cancellations screens) and the flight number without a space and in the same colour, starting at the same place), with the columns FLIGHT, STD (the scheduled time), ETD (the estimated time), STATUS and ATD (the actual time of departure, once the flight has left). All the flights of the day go on one page: with more than 6 the rows and the letters get smaller (down to 45%, 13 flights), and only with even more are there more pages, as even as possible (15 seconds each page). All the flights of the day stay on the screen, also those that have left (they are not taken off the list); only an hour after the departure of the last flight of the day does the screen show tomorrow's flights (once they are known; until then it keeps today's). The status and the ETD come from the airport's public FLIC page (TAP's, the same that gives the stand and ETA of the Gates page; asked every 5 minutes between 3 hours before the first flight and an hour after the last): its REMARKS column gives DEPARTED (pale blue), DELAYED (yellow), CANCELLED (red), BOARDING (white, blinking like a flashing sign: a cycle of 1.5 seconds in one step, on and then off) or BOARDED (white, with a tick to the right of the text), and its ETD and ATD columns give the estimated and the actual time of departure (the ETD is the scheduled time when it says nothing). Without a remark the status is the AeroDataBox's (ON TIME, or SCHEDULED when there is no live data; a flight that nobody says has left and whose time passed an hour ago counts as DEPARTED). Codeshares are left out; a delay counts from 15 minutes. The Pi remembers the flights of the day (in `/var/lib/skypaddock-hub/departures-day.json`), because neither source keeps them all day. It calls the AeroDataBox FIDS API with a private key and spreads the monthly quota over the days left.
- **Cancellations**: today's cancelled departures from the same airport, all airlines: flight, destination and scheduled time (a `?` marks a cancellation AeroDataBox is not sure about). It reuses the Departures responses, so it costs no extra calls; the bottom bar shows when the list was last refreshed.
- **Overhead**: aircraft around home, from the owner's ADS-B receiver (tar1090/readsb), completed by the open [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi) network (asked every 5 minutes; no daily quota, 1 request a second, personal non-commercial use) with adsb.lol as backup (500 requests a day per address; it has refused the Pi since 2 November 2026, and a source that answers 403 or 429 is left alone for 6 hours) and, for the aircraft on the ground at the airport, The OpenSky Network (which the other two cannot hear); routes from adsbdb by callsign. Metric units throughout. The page asks the Pi for the aircraft again each time its first page comes round, if the last answer is more than a minute old (the aircraft would otherwise be as old as the 10-minute refresh of the whole page; if the Pi does not answer, the last ones stay). Up to three pages, 12 seconds each:
  - **Closest Now**: the closest aircraft in the air: flight number, airline, route, distance and direction from home, altitude with a climbing or descending mark, speed, type and registration. A route is shown only if the aircraft is near the line between its origin and destination (adsbdb routes are by callsign and sometimes out of date); otherwise "Route unknown".
  - **Within 50 km**: radar with rings at 10, 25 and 50 km, the runways, the RWY 17 approach track ending on the threshold and the holding patterns over a faint map (CARTO Dark Matter tiles at 40% opacity, zoom 9, served by the Pi (`/tiles/dark_all/`, with its key and a disk cache) and credited in the corner; if they do not load the radar is as before), home as a dot and the airport's name. Each aircraft is a plane (the owner's picture, `images/overhead-plane.png`: Lufthansa in its own colours, the others its shape in sky blue, the closest in yellow, parked ones in grey) pointing where it flies, with its flight number and, under it, the route (e.g. FRA-ZRH), with the airline's logo above when there is one. Labels never overlap each other, the arrows or the ring numbers. A list by distance sits beside it.
  - **Ground**: the airport plan, turned so the main runway lies across the screen, with a north mark: runways, taxiways with their letters, aprons, buildings and stand numbers where they fit. Aircraft on the ground show the stand or taxiway they are on; aircraft landing or taking off are in yellow. The group's aircraft (the airlines of the Departures screen, by callsign) stay in grey on their stand, with their last flight number, after switching the transponder off, until the same aircraft (same ICAO address) shows up again, another aircraft parks on that stand, or 36 hours pass. A list in the empty corner under the runway shows the group's aircraft on a stand and when they arrived.
  - Flight numbers (LH1176) come from callsigns made of the airline code and digits (DLH1176). For the group's airlines, whose callsigns often have letters (DLH4AB), the Pi service matches the callsign, or else the aircraft (ICAO address or registration), with the arrivals and departures it already gets from AeroDataBox for the Departures screen (the same request now asks for both), so parked aircraft show the flight they arrived with. Other callsigns with letters (RYR10UB) do not give the flight number, so the callsign is shown.
  - The airport plan is made from OpenStreetMap data (ODbL, credited on screen) by a script (not in this repository) and kept on the Pi with the home position, so no place is written in the code.
- **Airport METAR**: the METAR of the airport set on the page (its ICAO code in the settings; the city in the titles of this screen, Departures and Cancellations comes from the code: the Pi service looks it up in OurAirports, `/airport.json?icao=`, and anywhere else, or when "City Departures" would not fit before the clock (17 letters), the titles are just "Airport" and "Departures"; there is no airport name option since 1.24.0), decoded into plain English (wind, visibility, weather, clouds, temperature, humidity, pressure, trend). Busy reports are split into pages (the page number, 1/3, is at the bottom right, not in the title); the raw report is shown when there is room. The TAF (the forecast) is not shown: only the METAR, since 1.43.1.
- **Work Schedule**: three pages (the third, Gates, is described at the end). A night shift that began yesterday and ends after midnight stays in the list and on the Next Shift page ("On shift, ends in...") until it has ended. The first page, Next Shift (9 seconds), shows the next (or current) shift with a countdown and the weather at its start and end; the second, Work Schedule (10 seconds), lists the shifts for the next 7 days with the weather at the start of each. The schedule is a private spreadsheet that a job on the Pi copies from cloud storage (read only) every hour; it never leaves the Pi. When the spreadsheet also has the "(R)" sheets of the months (same layout as the others), the (R) schedule of each day is shown in small under the shift, start and end of that day (the day column keeps its big text; the other columns get smaller to make room, and the rows have alternating bands). The option **Personal Schedule** (On by default) turns it off: with Off, or without the (R) sheets, every column has the big text in the whole height of the row, as before. **Gates** (third page): the flights of the owner's gates of the day, one row per flight in the order of departure, with the airline tail and the flight number (the ramp agent of the flight in the small letters of the bottom bar under it, `NAME`, when the roster names one), the staff on the gate (two names one under the other; three or more in pairs, `A - B` and the next line, to save height; the owner, when he is on the gate, is always the first name and in capitals, as the roster writes it: the Pi sends his name in `/schedule.json` (`me`, from `HORARIO_ME`), the site has no names in it; in work mode the names are as they come), the stand and the ETA centred in their columns, the ETA of the aircraft that does the rotation; under the ETA a countdown (`T-45`, in the small letters of the bottom bar), yellow and blinking slowly under 20 minutes, red and faster under 10 (worked out when the page is drawn). A gate the Pi marks (`star`: the owner and a colleague set on the Pi on the same gate) has a star beside its names (never in work mode: the star is the owner's). The bottom bar (the one of the other screens) names the supervisors of the morning, afternoon and night (`SPV: A - B`) and, when there are several pages, shows the page counter. All the flights go on one page: with more than 5 the rows and the letters get smaller (down to 60%, about 8 flights with two names), and only with even more are there more pages, as even as possible, 20 seconds each. A flight of today leaves the list once its departure time has passed (the departure from FLIC, or the time of the team file when FLIC has none); a flight with no time stays. Rita's gates (her name as the roster writes it, `HORARIO_RITA` on the Pi) are listed with the owner's, and her name, like the owner's, is in capitals: the owner's in the colour of the flight numbers (orange, `$av-yellow`), Rita's in green (the star stays the owner's); only with Personal Gates On: with it Off no name is highlighted. Two names on one line are in smaller letters (13 pt), so that long names are not cut. The yellow letters of the first page of the schedule (Next Shift: the date and the Start and End labels) are in the same orange. Once the last flight of today has left, or on a day without gates, the page shows the next day with gates of the owner, however far ahead it is within the 9 days of the schedule; the title says "Gates Today", "Gates Tomorrow", or from two days on the date ("Gates Fri 20/11"). When no day of the 9 has gates it says "No gates assigned", or "Day off" when the next day is off, and the page lasts 20 seconds like the others. The gates come from a team roster spreadsheet read on the Pi next to the schedule (it never leaves the Pi either); the stand and the ETA of today's flights (and the departure time, which only orders the rows) come from the two public FLIC pages of TAP for OPO, which the Pi service asks only on days with gates, around the flights, every 5 minutes (arrivals) and 15 minutes (departures). With the option **Personal Gates** (Settings) Off the page shows the gates of everybody instead ("All Gates", with the date of the day in the title as above): every flight of the day that has staff in the roster, each with the people who have its destination, the owner's or not (`allGates` in the data of the Pi); a day off of the owner then makes no difference, and the Pi service asks FLIC on every day that has such flights.
