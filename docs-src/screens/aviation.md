# Aviation screens

Navy and silver, with yellow flight numbers. Apart from the METAR, they read their data from a small service on the Raspberry Pi (see [The Raspberry Pi side](../../README.md#the-raspberry-pi-side)).

- **Airport METAR**: the METAR of the airport set on the page (its ICAO code in the settings; the city in the titles of this screen, Departures and Cancellations comes from the code: the Pi service looks it up in OurAirports, `/airport.json?icao=`, and anywhere else, or when "City Departures" would not fit before the clock (17 letters), the titles are just "Airport" and "Departures"; there is no airport name option since 1.24.0), decoded into plain English (wind, visibility, weather, clouds, temperature, humidity, pressure, trend). Busy reports are split into pages (the page number, 1/3, is at the bottom right, not in the title); the raw report is shown when there is room. On the Pi, the airport's TAF follows on its own pages: issue time, then each change group (Becoming, Temporary, Prob 30%, From) with its period in Portuguese time. aviationweather.gov does not accept browser requests, so the TAF comes through the Pi (`/taf.json`) and is left out anywhere else.
- **Departures**: next departures from the airport set on the Pi, for the airlines set there, with a tail icon, estimated time and status (ON TIME, DELAYED, CANCELLED, or SCHEDULED when there is no live data). Codeshares are left out; a delay counts from 15 minutes. The Pi calls the AeroDataBox FIDS API with a private key and spreads the monthly quota over the days left.
- **Cancellations**: today's cancelled departures from the same airport, all airlines: flight, destination and scheduled time (a `?` marks a cancellation AeroDataBox is not sure about). It reuses the Departures responses, so it costs no extra calls; the bottom bar shows when the list was last refreshed.
- **Overhead**: aircraft around home, from the owner's ADS-B receiver (tar1090/readsb), completed by the open [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi) network (asked every 5 minutes; no daily quota, 1 request a second, personal non-commercial use) with adsb.lol as backup (500 requests a day per address; it has refused the Pi since 2 November 2026, and a source that answers 403 or 429 is left alone for 6 hours) and, for the aircraft on the ground at the airport, The OpenSky Network (which the other two cannot hear); routes from adsbdb by callsign. Metric units throughout. Up to three pages, 12 seconds each:
  - **Closest Now**: the closest aircraft in the air: flight number, airline, route, distance and direction from home, altitude with a climbing or descending mark, speed, type and registration. A route is shown only if the aircraft is near the line between its origin and destination (adsbdb routes are by callsign and sometimes out of date); otherwise "Route unknown".
  - **Within 50 km**: radar with rings at 10, 25 and 50 km, the runways, the RWY 17 approach track ending on the threshold and the holding patterns over a faint map (CARTO Dark Matter tiles at 40% opacity, zoom 9, served by the Pi (`/tiles/dark_all/`, with its key and a disk cache) and credited in the corner; if they do not load the radar is as before), home as a dot and the airport's name. Each aircraft is a plane (the owner's picture, `images/overhead-plane.png`: Lufthansa in its own colours, the others its shape in sky blue, the closest in yellow, parked ones in grey) pointing where it flies, with its flight number and, under it, the route (e.g. FRA-ZRH), with the airline's logo above when there is one. Labels never overlap each other, the arrows or the ring numbers. A list by distance sits beside it.
  - **Ground**: the airport plan, turned so the main runway lies across the screen, with a north mark: runways, taxiways with their letters, aprons, buildings and stand numbers where they fit. Aircraft on the ground show the stand or taxiway they are on; aircraft landing or taking off are in yellow. The group's aircraft (the airlines of the Departures screen, by callsign) stay in grey on their stand, with their last flight number, after switching the transponder off, until the same aircraft (same ICAO address) shows up again, another aircraft parks on that stand, or 36 hours pass. A list in the empty corner under the runway shows the group's aircraft on a stand and when they arrived.
  - Flight numbers (LH1176) come from callsigns made of the airline code and digits (DLH1176). For the group's airlines, whose callsigns often have letters (DLH4AB), the Pi service matches the callsign, or else the aircraft (ICAO address or registration), with the arrivals and departures it already gets from AeroDataBox for the Departures screen (the same request now asks for both), so parked aircraft show the flight they arrived with. Other callsigns with letters (RYR10UB) do not give the flight number, so the callsign is shown.
  - The airport plan is made from OpenStreetMap data (ODbL, credited on screen) by a script (not in this repository) and kept on the Pi with the home position, so no place is written in the code.
- **Work Schedule**: three pages (the third, Gates, is described at the end). A night shift that began yesterday and ends after midnight stays in the list and on the Next Shift page ("On shift, ends in...") until it has ended. The first page, Next Shift (7 seconds), shows the next (or current) shift with a countdown and the weather at its start and end; the second, Work Schedule (10 seconds), lists the shifts for the next 7 days with the weather at the start of each. The schedule is a private spreadsheet that a job on the Pi copies from cloud storage (read only) every hour; it never leaves the Pi. When the spreadsheet also has the "(R)" sheets of the months (same layout as the others), the (R) schedule of each day is shown in small under the shift, start and end of that day (the day column keeps its big text; the other columns get smaller to make room, and the rows have alternating bands). The option **Personal Schedule** (On by default) turns it off: with Off, or without the (R) sheets, every column has the big text in the whole height of the row, as before. **Gates** (third page): the flights of the owner's gates of the day, one row per flight in the order of departure, with the airline tail and the flight number (the ramp agent of the flight in the small letters of the bottom bar under it, `NAME`, when the roster names one), the staff on the gate (two names one under the other; three or more in pairs, `A - B` and the next line, to save height), the stand and the ETA centred in their columns, the ETA of the aircraft that does the rotation; under the ETA a countdown (`T-45`, in the small letters of the bottom bar), yellow and blinking slowly under 20 minutes, red and faster under 10 (worked out when the page is drawn). A gate the Pi marks (`star`: the owner and a colleague set on the Pi on the same gate) has a star beside its names. The bottom bar (the one of the other screens) names the supervisors of the morning, afternoon and night (`SPV: A - B`) and, with more than 6 flights, shows the page counter: the flights go on 2 or more pages, as even as possible (9 flights: 5 and 4), 10 seconds each. The rows get smaller only when the flights of a page do not fit. A flight of today leaves the list once its departure time has passed (the departure from FLIC, or the time of the team file when FLIC has none); a flight with no time stays. Once the last flight of today has left, or on a day without gates, the page shows the next day with gates of the owner, however far ahead it is within the 9 days of the schedule; the title says "Gates Today", "Gates Tomorrow", or from two days on the date ("Gates Fri 20/11"). When no day of the 9 has gates it says "No gates assigned", or "Day off" when the next day is off, and the page lasts 10 seconds like the others. The gates come from a team roster spreadsheet read on the Pi next to the schedule (it never leaves the Pi either); the stand and the ETA of today's flights (and the departure time, which only orders the rows) come from the two public FLIC pages of TAP for OPO, which the Pi service asks only on days with gates, around the flights, every 5 minutes (arrivals) and 15 minutes (departures). With the option **Personal Gates** (Settings) Off the page shows the gates of everybody instead ("All Gates", with the date of the day in the title as above): every flight of the day that has staff in the roster, each with the people who have its destination, the owner's or not (`allGates` in the data of the Pi); a day off of the owner then makes no difference, and the Pi service asks FLIC on every day that has such flights.
