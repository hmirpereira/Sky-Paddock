# Formula 1 screens

- **Titles**: the word F1 in the titles (F1 Drivers, F1 Next Race, F1 Telemetry, F1 Race and Sprint Grid, F1 Last Race and Last Sprint, and the F1 News page) is the Formula 1 logo, supplied by the owner, without the TM mark: white on the F1 screens (`images/f1-logo.svg`), in its own red, with a thin white outline, on the F1 News page, whose title is just "F1 News" in one line (like "Latest News"; the second line of the F1 screens' titles, such as "Round 19", is set a little smaller, and the Verstappen Jet's says "Tracker") (`images/f1-logo-red.svg`; `utils/f1-title.mjs`).
Red and black.

- **F1 Photo** (Pi only, off by default; add `f1-photo-checkbox=true` to the page address): one picture an hour, chosen at random from the top of the week and of the month of r/F1Porn, shown in a red frame that takes about 95% of the screen, with the title of the post over the bottom of the picture (the resolution that posts usually carry, such as `(1920x1080)`, is removed). It follows F1 Last Race and is skipped when the Pi has no picture. The Pi service (`hub_f1photo.py`) reads the public Atom feed of the subreddit (the JSON is refused to anonymous clients and is not worked around), keeps only image posts hosted on `i.redd.it`, downloads a new original every Lisbon hour (two feed requests an hour; the page picks it up on its next 10 minute refresh) and serves it as `f1photo.jpg` with `f1photo.json`. Pictures over 6 MB are skipped, and JPEGs over 12 megapixels are shrunk by the hub to 2560 px on the long side (Pillow; PNGs over 30 megapixels are skipped; the Pi 3 has little memory; `F1PHOTO_MAX_BYTES`, `F1PHOTO_MAX_PIXELS`), the previous picture is not repeated, and `F1PHOTO=0` turns it off. No NSFW flag exists in the feed, so the subreddit's rules are what keeps it safe. The pictures belong to their authors and are shown unaltered with no credit line.
- **F1 Last Race**: the latest Grand Prix or sprint ("F1 Last Sprint") with flag and date, a podium with team icons, the winner's time and the gaps for 2nd and 3rd, and the fastest lap.
- **F1 Grid**: the starting order of the next sprint or race in two staggered columns, like a real grid, scrolling through the field. It is the qualifying classification, so grid penalties are not included. Pole shows its lap time and the others the gap to it, from the last part of qualifying each driver reached ("No time" if they set none there).
- **Weekend timing** (`utils/f1-weekend.mjs`): the two screens take turns. After sprint qualifying, sprint grid until the sprint; after the sprint, sprint result until qualifying; after qualifying, race grid until the race; after the race, race result until the next weekend starts. Results are asked for shortly after each session, then every 20 minutes until they appear, and once more at the next F1 update time to catch penalties.
- **Sources**: Jolpica F1 first (its alpha API for sprint qualifying); OpenF1 when Jolpica has not published a session yet, which can take until after the weekend (`utils/f1-openf1.mjs`).
- **F1 Drivers**: the whole drivers' championship, 7 rows at a time, scrolling like the original Travel Forecast, with a team icon per driver (each driver's current team comes from the latest race).
- **F1 Next Race**: skipped when the race has no dated session yet. Page 1 has the track outline, Grand Prix, circuit, place with an 8-bit flag, track length, date and countdown; page 2 has the weekend schedule in Portuguese time and the forecast at the circuit for the start of the race (from 15 days before). Outlines are in `utils/f1-circuits.mjs`. Every circuit has its lap record ("TRACK RECORD": time, driver, year) in place of the circuit name, and the circuits whose corners or sections have a name also get a dot and the name on the outline (Spa, Monaco, Monza, Silverstone, Suzuka, Zandvoort, Mexico City, Montreal, Interlagos); a corner with no name is not marked, not even with its number, and there is no start line or arrow (their positions could not be verified). All of it lives in `utils/circuit-facts.mjs`, written by hand from public sources (the race lap record as in the Wikipedia article of the circuit, to be checked each season); the position of each corner was checked against the real positions on the circuit (the outlines agree with them to 2 to 9 m). Madring, Sepang, Istanbul and Portimao only have the record. `utils/circuit-view.mjs` sizes the map so the names always fit.
- **F1 Telemetry**: the best laps of the two fastest drivers in the latest practice or qualifying session (sprint qualifying included), from OpenF1's car data (speed about 4 times a second) and positions. Page 1: lap number, lap time, gap and sector times (the faster sector on a chip of the driver's colour), the 25 mini-sectors as a strip in lap order and how many each driver won, and the track drawn from the fastest driver's positions, each mini-sector in the colour of the driver who was faster there, with the start line (labelled S1), the direction of the lap and the S2 and S3 lines; the labels always stay inside the map (on the other side of the track, or pushed in, when they would be cut off). Page 2: speed along the lap for both, and under it the gap between them along the lap (filled in the colour of the driver ahead), with the sector lines. The distance along the lap comes from integrating the speed, so both laps are put on the same scale, and the second lap is stretched piece by piece so its sector lines fall where the fastest lap's do: the gap is exact at the sector lines and the end, and approximate between them; small differences in the line taken are ignored. The screen looks for a newer session at most once an hour, 30 minutes after a session ends (OpenF1 refuses free requests while a session is live), and keeps the latest result in `localStorage`. Driver colours are the team colours from OpenF1, made lighter when needed to read on the red background; when both are close (same team, or teams with similar colours) the second driver gets a lighter tint. All F1 screens ask OpenF1 through one queue (`utils/f1-openf1.mjs`), one request at a time and spaced, because it refuses requests that come too close together (429). The screen is skipped three days after the end of the weekend it is about (the latest session of the same meeting, the race usually), and shows again with the next weekend's first practice.
- **Verstappen Jet**: Max Verstappen's private jet (PH-UTL, a Falcon 8X, ICAO address 4867E6). Until version 1.3 the data came from F1 Jet Tracker, which now lets only its own site read its files. The Pi service serves `f1jet.json` and keeps the last flight. The world map is Natural Earth 1:10m simplified into `utils/world-map.mjs` by a script (not in this repository). The sources and their limits are in [Sources and limits](../sources.md).
  - **Page 1**: a boarding pass of the last flight (three-letter airport codes such as TZX-NCE, cities, times in Lisbon, duration and date), with the MV logo on the route line at the jet's place and a lion in the background (both supplied by the owner of this project), and where the jet is now: how long it has been on the ground, or altitude and speed in the air, with the destination when it is known and "Took off from XXX" when it is not.
  - **Page 2**: a map of that flight's route from its ADS-B positions, with the two airports; while flying, a dot marks the jet. Any stretch with no positions (a jump of more than 3 degrees, as over Central America and the Amazon, where no receiver sees the jet) is drawn dashed, never as a solid track. Without a track the map shows a straight line between the two airports.
  - **Where the jet is now**: the Pi service asks the open data of [adsb.fi](https://adsb.fi) (personal, non-commercial use; 1 request a second at most; cited on the screen), once an hour and every 5 minutes while it flies, to catch the landing. A source that refuses is left alone for 6 hours. The order is:
    1. adsb.fi.
    2. [airplanes.live](https://airplanes.live) (same format), when adsb.fi does not see the jet.
    3. [AvioADSB](https://avioadsb.org) (same format, CC BY-NC 4.0, cited on the screen), only with the owner's free key `AVIOADSB_KEY` in the Pi's configuration (10,000 requests a month, so only when the other two do not see the jet). Its failures are not shown as an error of the jet, and a 429 or a server error pauses it for 10 minutes.
    4. The OpenSky Network's live states, when none of them sees the jet.
    Until 2 November 2026 this came from the open adsb.lol network, which now answers the Pi with "banned" (respected: it is not asked for 6 hours after a refusal).
  - **Flying out of coverage**: a flight lost high in the air (out of coverage over the Pacific or South America) with no arrival counts as still flying for up to 12 hours, instead of showing "?" as the destination. A last position low (under 4,000 ft) near an airport, or on the ground, gives the arrival.
  - **The route**: the track files of adsb.lol, which gave the route of the last hours, are now behind a verification page for anything but a browser; that is not worked around, they are asked again only every 6 hours, and the route comes from The OpenSky Network. Flights older than about a day come from The OpenSky Network with the owner's free account (flights by aircraft, worked out overnight, and their tracks; checked at start-up up to 30 days back and then every 6 hours).
  - **Airports**: a flight is a stretch in the air between two stretches on the ground, and its airports are the nearest ones (within 12 km, from a monthly copy of OurAirports) to the ground points or to the low points of the climb and descent. When OpenSky loses the jet (over the Atlantic, for instance) and gives no arrival airport, the arrival is the airport nearest to the end of the route or, after landing, to where the jet is seen on the ground (within 24 h of take-off), and the landing time is the first sighting there. When the departure airport is only known afterwards and the route starts far from it (a track that begins already in the air), the line of the map starts at that airport.
  - **Airports still missing**, in this order:
    1. [Celebplanes](https://www.celebplanes.com/tail/PH-UTL): the Pi service reads the public flight list of PH-UTL, with its permission, once a day at most and only while the last flight lacks an airport (a track that starts already in the air has no departure airport), and takes the airports of the flight that landed at the same time (within 20 minutes).
    2. AeroDataBox, which has a monthly quota and is asked only for what is still missing (the departure airport too): the Pi service asks for the jet's flights by registration and takes the arrival airport and time of the one that left at the same time (from the flight plan). It is used when OpenSky loses the jet over Mexico or the Atlantic and adsb.lol does not see it on the ground: one request per flight, a second one 3 hours later if there was no answer, only for flights of the last 3 days, counted in the same monthly quota as Departures.
    If OpenSky later sees the real landing, its airport wins.
- **Update rule** (`utils/f1-update.mjs`): F1 Drivers and F1 Next Race download their data once a day from Friday to Monday after 22:00 (Lisbon time) and keep it in `localStorage` the rest of the week.
- **Racing gap** (`utils/racing-gap.mjs`, `utils/racing-gap-events.mjs`): when no F1 or GT World event starts in the next 21 days and the last one ended more than 72 hours ago, the F1 screens (Last Race, Grid, Telemetry, Next Race and Standings) are skipped; the two GT screens follow the GT calendar alone (no GT event in the next 21 days and the last one over 72 hours ago), whatever F1 does. The Verstappen Jet always shows, so in the gap it is the only F1 screen. A GT event with dates but no dated sessions yet counts from its dates. The events come from the F1 schedule and the Pi's `gt.json`; if either is not known, nothing is skipped.
